Kalshi Combo Lab

A Combo is a single contract with its own quote. The product of individual probabilities is only an independence benchmark and is not a valid same-game fair price.

For the joint probability, use a validated correlation model trained on pregame information. Do not multiply same-game probabilities and call the result a forecast.

Data that changes the decision

These inputs matter more than streaks and “expert consensus.”

Team-level model

EPA/play, success rate, early downs, explosive plays, pass rush vs pressure allowed, offense vs defensive formation, opponent-adjusted pace, travel and rest.

Player prop model

Routes, snaps, rush share, target share, red-zone touches, quarterback status, opponent coverage, game script and realistic playing time.

Market validation

Exact settlement language, YES ask, liquidity/slippage, maker/taker fees, joint-event correlation, out-of-sample calibration, ROI and closing price.

Recommended workflow

  1. Night before: update completed games, EPA and opponent strength. Freeze a versioned forecast.
  2. Gameday: update injury designations, projected snaps/usage, stadium wind, confirmed QBs and depth-chart changes.
  3. At inactive deadline: re-run affected projections. Record sources and their timestamps.
  4. Just before entry: fetch an executable Kalshi quote only for shortlisted exact contracts. Record actual total fees and rules.
  5. After close: capture closing prices and final outcomes; compare calibration, ROI and CLV by league and market.
  6. Use a PASS state when data quality is poor or the probability range crosses break-even.

The present decision lab is a manual front end. It does not fetch current scores, injuries or market quotes, and does not itself train predictive models.
